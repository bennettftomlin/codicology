# Audit of six books against their PDFs — 2026-11-11

Six installed EPUBs of different kinds were read against their PDFs to find defects nobody had
noticed. All six were built on 10 or 11 November, so they show what the code does now (one exception is
noted under K). Nothing was rebuilt or changed: a conversion was running in Calibre during the audit.

| id | book | kind | pages read against the page image |
|---|---|---|---|
| 936 | Principles of Microeconomics | typeset textbook: tables, charts, equations | about 40 |
| 1112 | FM 4-25 First Aid | typeset illustrated manual: steps, warning boxes, captioned drawings | 35 |
| 2061 | Burning Up | typeset monograph: endnotes, tables, bibliography, index | 37, and the 11 left-out pages |
| 1124 | Where There Is No Doctor | freely laid-out handbook: text round drawings, hand-lettered charts | 46 |
| 5493 | The Knights of Industry | scan, nineteenth century: prose, footnotes, facsimiles | 41 |
| 9564 | Studies in the History of Accounting | scan, 1956: dense footnotes, ledger tables, six languages | 38 |

**How.** Each book had one reviewer (a subagent), a stratified sample of 29–37 pages (front matter,
chapter openings, tables, pictures, notes, lists, index, bibliography, thin pages, random pages) and a
list of mechanical leads. I then checked the main findings myself and, where a finding could be counted,
counted it across the shelf from the caches or the installed EPUBs. Each finding below says which of
those it has had: **seen** (I looked at the page or the markup), **counted** (measured across books),
or **reported** (the reviewer's word only).

The tools, samples, leads and census outputs are in
`/Users/Bennett1/claude_knowledge/rebuilds/2026-11-11-audit/` (`tools/`, `work/<id>/`, `BRIEF.md`).

Running prose is good in every book. The two scan reviewers read about 880 lines closely against the
image and found 2 errors; the typeset books matched their text layers on every prose page compared.
The defects are in handwriting, notes, tables, boxes, symbols and structure.

## A. Invented text

### A1. Handwriting and manuscript pages are read as type, and the words are made up — seen, counted
- **5493**: pp. 102, 104, 114, 115, 174, 175, 188 are facsimiles of handwritten letters and
  signatures. The EPUB has no picture of any of them and prints invented text, often in the wrong
  language. p. 175, a German letter printed sideways: "I should should I will there should / I should I
  / I should I / I should I". p. 188: the letterhead is read, the body is "If forth will fire Ifor
  shittance…", signed "C. Glaves" where the page is signed "L. Lehnert".
- **5493 p. 292**, a full-page newspaper advertisement, is kept as a picture and followed by about 25
  paragraphs that are not on the page ("The Commission of the State of Virginia is to appoint a
  Commissioner of the State of Virginia…").
- **9564 pp. 13–16** (plates V–VIII, photographs of manuscript ledgers): no picture, or a scrap of one;
  an Arabic word, hundreds of dashes, "The state of the County of New York is a state of the United
  States of America", "The Compass Central Park is at 36° 18' N"; three of the four captions lost.
  (Reported; I checked the cached text and confidence of two, not the page images.)
- **The recogniser knows.** Its own confidence is cached for every page (median 0.995 over 38,250
  pages). In 5493 the seven lowest-confidence pages are seven of these eight (0.75–0.84); the eighth,
  the signatures page, is 0.92. Low confidence alone is not the signal: half-titles and one-line pages
  score low too. Low confidence **with a real amount of text** is: 15 pages on the shelf pair a mean
  under 0.86 with 30 or more words, in 5493 (7), 1113 (4, engineering drawings), 9564 (3), 3663 (1).
  I have looked at the 5493 ones only.

### A2. The recogniser describes the page instead of reading it — counted, not looked at on the page
- 8767: "The faint text block contains multiple lines of text, likely containing a revision or…" (twice).
- 3663: "An image of a potato stamen with a purple cen…".
- 9564 p. 13: "[Handwritten notes and a small circle of a star]".
- 5498: "[Redacted]" three times; 5493 p. 292: "[redacted]"; 5298: "CLERK [REDACTED]".
A search of every cached reading for such phrases found these and no others that are not the book's
own words (the other 490 matches are sentences like "an image of impartiality").

## B. Wrong words on typeset pages whose own text layer has the right ones — seen
- **1124 p. 413, a dose.** The page prints "children 9 to 14 years: 1 ½ tablets (150 mg.)". The EPUB
  has "½ tablets". The recogniser dropped the 1; the PDF's text layer has it.
- **1124 p. 72.** The third fever chart is HEPATITIS; the EPUB labels it TYPHOID, as it does the
  second.
- **1124 pp. 4 and 7.** In the contents, "Beliefs That Can Make People Well 2" and "Meningitis 185"
  are gone and the rows below them appear twice.
In all four the text layer is right. The reconciler takes the layer's word only in a run of up to three
words with the same number of words on each side, so a dropped word or a replaced row is outside it.
The mechanical check counts, per book, layer words the EPUB does not have; most of them are text inside
pictures, and the rest have not been separated out.

## D. The same thing printed two or three times

### D2. Table cells printed again as pictures — seen, counted

## F. Bullets and dashes turned into characters no reader can show — seen, counted
Where a PDF sets bullets in a symbol font, its text layer holds a private-use or control character, and
the reconciler takes that over the bullet the recogniser read correctly.
935: U+F0B7 × 190. 2031: U+F06C × 383, U+F06E × 14. 1110: U+0083 × 164, U+F8E7 × 4 (a dash).
1112: U+0090 × 4 (the star on its cover pages, and "indicated by a star ()").

## A second reader

Tesseract cannot check a table or an equation, and a scan has no text layer. LightOnOCR-2 (1.1 GB,
on the llama-server already installed) was tried as a second generative reader. On twelve pages
Surya had got wrong it had eight right, shared one error, and on the three unreadable pages
invented as Surya does, but differently. It is not a replacement (it gives no layout) and it is
used only where something else can prefer it, or where the two readings differ in a way that
points one way:

- **Tables** (`convert --second-reader`): on the 26 typeset pages whose tables had lost the most,
  Surya's reading held 78% of the 1,662 figures the PDF prints and the second reader's 91%. A table
  short of the PDF's figures is replaced by the second reader's where that holds nearly all of
  them: six tables on those pages, none with a figure the PDF does not print. Table 12 of *Burning
  Up* goes from 31 to 53 of 55 figures: it had three of its six columns of figures and has all six.
- **Equations**: on a typeset page the PDF's symbols choose. On a scan the second reader's equation
  is taken only where the two differ in digits alone and it has no fewer. Over 118 pages and 247
  equations that changes three (20/100 and 1/3 in Sraffa, k/m in Augie), each rightly, and lists
  fourteen where the first is kept, in the build log and in a `.second.txt` beside the cache. Of
  fourteen blocks on which the two readers differ in Sraffa, looked at one by one, the second
  reader is right on nine, the first on two, and three are mixed. Neither Tesseract nor Apple
  Vision can read a built-up fraction, so nothing installed can settle the ones that are listed.
- **Disputed words** (`adjudicate --second-reader`): a dispute left open is settled as a count of
  readers (Apple Vision and the second reader: one with a view, or both agreeing, never the two
  against each other), and a verdict of the vocabulary or the dictionary that the second reader
  reads the other way is withdrawn. Measured on 136 disputes between Surya's first reading and
  Tesseract on 207 born-digital pages, with the publisher's text as the answer: of the 47 the ladder
  left open, the count of readers settled 31, none wrongly, where Vision alone settled 34 and four
  wrongly; the second reader contradicted ten verdicts of the vocabulary and the dictionary, nine of
  them wrong and one that could not be judged. On blurred renders of three more books (75 pages, 20
  disputes with Tesseract) it sided with the publisher's text in 17 of the 18 that could be judged;
  the other is a page whose own text layer holds "l'm" for "I'm".
  On a real scan, Sraffa (122 pages, 313 disputes on 105 of them, 22 minutes to read those pages):
  the ladder had proposed 12 changes to the shipped text and now proposes 5. The seven that went
  were each a misreading of damaged type offered in place of a right word ("barm" for harm,
  "Deficil-industries", "untike", "caling", "It" for the B of Appendix B, and two subscripts); the
  four that are real errors of the first reader are all still proposed (facit for tacit, IRINITY,
  substituence for subsistence, resolve for restore). Eight disputes the ladder had left open are
  settled, all rightly. One right verdict was withdrawn for nothing: the second reader read "alsa"
  for an italic "also", as the ink has it.

A correction to something said during the work: Table 12's "74.6%" is what the page prints. The
second reader copied it faithfully.

**A third reader, added later the same day.** GLM-OCR reads regions, so it can be shown the one
equation two readers differ on and asked for the formula (under a second each). The equation printed
is then the reading that has another reader on its side at every point where it differs. On the
fourteen equations of Sraffa on which the first two readers differ, judged against the page: twelve
come out as printed (nine from the second reader, two that are the third's own because the first
and the second each had one symbol wrong, one kept as the first had it), two stay as the first
reader had them and are wrong, and none is made worse. The digits-only rule had settled two. On
typeset pages, where the PDF is the answer, one majority was wrong: it had lost a digit. So a
reading with fewer figures than the first reader's is never printed on a vote.

## 12 November: finding B, and what it turned out to be

Finding B (a dose printed "1 ½ tablets" and read "½ tablets") was left on 11 November as a list a
build prints: twenty figures on the shelf that the PDF's text has between two words the reading runs
together. Looked at on the page, five of the twenty are real (the dose, the year in "Temas de hoy,
1996, p. 521", and three page references in one table of *Where There Is No Doctor*) and fifteen
are the layer's own order.

The pages showed the same loss in ordinary words. The recogniser writes a doubled word once, takes
the second of two like words for the first ("1.4 billion toe to 2.5 billion", "as well as fora for
teaching"), and twice dropped a whole line between two words that look alike. The reconciler now
puts such words back (`28369d3`) where three words agree on each side of the gap, each word is a
word or a figure, the page does not have it already, and in the PDF it stands in the line.

Measured by running the real build as far as the reconciler on all 134 books with born-digital
pages (27,779 such pages): 38 places, 59 words, in 28 books, and nothing in the other 106. Each of
the 38 was looked at on the page. Fifteen are a doubled word the book prints, some of them the
book's own slips, which are put back as printed. Eighteen figures stay on the list: fifteen that
the page has elsewhere, and the three page references (*Where There Is No Doctor*, PDF page 95),
which the page does lack but which stand in a column of their own.

The 28 books were rebuilt and each compared with its installed copy
(`rebuilds/2026-11-12-put-back/`); the words put back are the only words that changed, but for the
last word of one page in each of two books whose installed copies dated from September.
